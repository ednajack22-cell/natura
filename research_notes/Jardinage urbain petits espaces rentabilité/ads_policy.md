# Display-ad monetization and Google policy for a small-space food-growing site (Natura)

Research date: 2026-10-23. Scope: US first, then UK. Sources fetched directly where possible; figures that come only from search summaries or self-reported bloggers are flagged as such.

## 1. How valuable is home & garden / food-growing traffic for display ads (RPM/CPM, seasonality)?

### Takeaway
No ad network (Mediavine, Raptive, Ezoic, Journey) publishes official RPM figures by niche. The only figures available are self-reported by bloggers. They place "home & garden" among the better-paying lifestyle niches (one dated data point: about $39 page RPM on Mediavine in February 2022), but no reliable, recent, gardening-specific RPM benchmark exists. Q4 is the strongest quarter for ad rates and Q1 the weakest. For gardening, that ad-rate cycle runs against the traffic cycle, which peaks in spring. No published data quantifies this.

### Cited Findings
- **Home/Garden RPM (one data point, dated).** Fat Stacks (Jon Dykstra, who runs several sites on the same theme and settings, all aimed at the US) reported Mediavine ad RPM for 18–24 Feb 2022:
  - Home/Garden $39.10 ($33.24 at the regular revenue share)
  - Fashion $30.44
  - Travel $25.53
  - Parenting $23.40
  - Entertainment $14.12
  - Tech $11.88

  He treats ≥$25 as a good niche RPM and says under $10 "chances are something is wrong (e.g., non-US traffic)". — [Fat Stacks, "What's a Good Ad RPM?", published Sept 5, 2022](https://fatstacksblog.com/good-ad-rpm/)
- **Caveat on that figure.** It covers one week of one blogger's site in 2022, and the site was mostly home décor rather than food growing. It should not be used as a forecast for Natura. — same source.
- **Home & Garden is a mainstream premium-network niche.** In a sample of "hundreds of sites", Home & Garden was the second most common niche on Mediavine/Raptive after Food/Recipes. Within gardening, sites on indoor plants, herbs and hydroponics are cited as successful. The niche is described as "highly commercial with a large number of relevant products to recommend", which also helps affiliate income. — [The Website Flip, "Profitable niches from Mediavine and Raptive sites"](https://thewebsiteflip.com/guide/profitable-niches-raptive-mediavine/)
- **Revenue per visitor by niche.** In data from 500+ Motion Invest site listings, the niches with high revenue per visitor include "personal finance, tech products, beauty, home, and pets". — [The Website Flip (citing Motion Invest data)](https://thewebsiteflip.com/guide/profitable-niches-raptive-mediavine/)
- **Seasonality from a home-organisation blogger (Mediavine, 5-year income report, 2026).**
  - Ad rates rise in Q4 and at the end of each quarter. Q1 and the start of each quarter tend to pay least.
  - The blogger has also seen a "steady decline in RPMs over time", which she believes is common in the current ad market.
  - Her Pinterest traffic spikes in Q4 (gift guides) and dips in spring.

  — [Minimize My Mess, "5-Year Blog Income Report"](https://www.minimizemymess.com/blog/5-year-blog-income-report)
- **Q4 premium.** An aggregator states that Q4 typically earns 30–50% more than other quarters. This is an unverified third-party estimate; I found no network-published figure. — [This Week in Blogging, Mediavine vs Raptive](https://thisweekinblogging.com/mediavine-vs-raptive/) (search summary; not fetched)
- **Revenue share and payment terms.**
  - Mediavine pays a base 75% of gross revenue, and 90% for "Premiere" publishers (CEO Eric Hochberger, Reddit AMA, Sept 2025).
  - Journey runs on separate exchange seats without Google AdX demand, so it performs below standard Mediavine.

  — [PPC Land, Sept 18, 2025](https://ppc.land/mediavine-ceo-addresses-ai-challenges-and-journey-expansion-in-reddit-ama/)
- **Network comparison ranges.** Third-party 2026 comparison sites quote ranges such as Mediavine $22–45 and Raptive $28–52 across niches, and Food & Recipe at Mediavine $18–26 and Raptive $20–28. The sources are inconsistent with each other and give no methodology, so these ranges are **not reliable**. — [Earnifyhub](https://earnifyhub.com/blog/blogging/mediavine-vs-raptive-vs-ezoic-rpm); [Stacked Daily](https://www.stackeddaily.org/raptive-vs-mediavine-vs-adsense-2026)
- **Food blog scale example.** Midwest Foodie (Raptive) reported >$530k total income in 2025 with ~700k+ monthly views and 624 posts. This is a food/recipe blog, not gardening, and total income is not the same as ad RPM. — [Midwest Foodie income report 2025](https://midwestfoodieblog.com/how-i-made-over-530k-from-my-food-blog-in-2025/) (search summary; not fetched)

### Inferences
- Home & garden content attracts home-improvement, garden-retail and food/CPG advertisers. The available anecdotes place it in the middle-to-upper tier of lifestyle niches, below finance and above tech and entertainment.
- RPM depends heavily on the share of US/UK traffic. A US-first strategy is therefore consistent with maximising RPM.
- Food-growing traffic is likely to peak in spring (seed starting and planting), while ad rates peak in Q4 and bottom out in Q1. Autumn and winter content can help smooth the year: indoor and windowsill growing, microgreens, herbs indoors, gift guides for gardeners, and preserving the harvest (see section 4 for food safety).
- Mobile Pinterest traffic usually monetises below search traffic. I found no published figure for this, so it is an inference only.

### Gaps
- No official network data on RPM by niche, and no gardening-only income report with RPM figures was found.
- No published data quantifies spring traffic against Q1/Q2 RPM for gardening sites.
- No UK-specific RPM data. UK traffic is generally described as "Tier 1", but no figure comparing UK and US RPM was found.

## 2. Ad network entry requirements (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
- **AdSense** has no traffic minimum. It requires original content that meets its policies, and an applicant aged 18 or over.
- **Journey by Mediavine** requires 1,000 sessions from Tier 1 countries in 30 days.
- **Mediavine** requires $5,000 in annual ad revenue (this replaced the old 50,000-session rule).
- **Raptive** requires 25,000 monthly pageviews (since Oct 2025), with geography and quality conditions.
- **Ezoic** now requires 250,000+ monthly active users for new sites (since 19 Feb 2026), so it is no longer an entry-level option.

### Cited Findings
- **AdSense** — no traffic threshold. You need "your own content that meets our policies" and must be "18 or over". Content "must be high-quality, original, and attract an audience", and you must have access to the site's HTML. — [AdSense Help, Eligibility requirements](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724)
- **AdSense site readiness.**
  - Pages need "enough unique content" for Google to determine what the site is about.
  - Ads may not be placed on scraped or copied content, including content "modif[ied] slightly ... using automated techniques".
  - Sites should follow Google Search spam policies: no doorway pages and no thin pages.

  — [AdSense Help, content and user experience](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/10015918)
- **Mediavine (main program).** Sites must:
  - generate a minimum of $5,000 in annual ad revenue
  - publish original, audience-first content
  - maintain clean, human, brand-safe traffic
  - be in good standing with Google AdSense and AdExchange
  - provide a reader experience that supports premium advertising

  — [Mediavine Requirements page (fetched Oct 2026)](https://www.mediavine.com/mediavine-requirements/)
- **Journey by Mediavine.** Sites must:
  - receive a minimum of 1,000 sessions from Tier 1 countries (U.S., Canada, U.K., Australia) within 30 days
  - publish original, audience-first content
  - meet Mediavine content quality and traffic standards

  — [Mediavine Requirements page](https://www.mediavine.com/mediavine-requirements/)
- **Journey history.** The threshold was reported as 10,000 sessions before being lowered to 1,000. One source dates the change to 15 Jan 2026, but PPC Land (Sept 2025) already quotes the CEO saying Journey "accepts applications with as few as 1,000 monthly sessions". Treat the change date as unconfirmed. Journey also added ID verification for new applications in July 2025. — [PPC Land, Sept 18, 2025](https://ppc.land/mediavine-ceo-addresses-ai-challenges-and-journey-expansion-in-reddit-ama/); [Productive Blogging on Journey](https://www.productiveblogging.com/?p=15446) (search summary)
- **Journey revenue share.** One secondary source says Journey has a lower revenue share (70%) and requires running Mediavine's Grow tool for 30 days. This is not verified on Mediavine's own page. — [search summary of Journey reviews, e.g. bloggingguide.com](https://bloggingguide.com/?p=275365)
- **Raptive.** On 16 Oct 2025 Raptive cut its minimum from 100,000 to 25,000 monthly pageviews and retired the "Rise" (50k) tier. Other conditions:
  - Sites with 25k–99,999 pageviews need ≥50% of traffic from the US, UK, Canada, New Zealand or Australia. Sites at 100k+ need ≥40%.
  - Most pages must be long-form.
  - Content must be original and high-quality, with "meaningful human involvement".
  - Google Analytics must be set up correctly.
  - The domain must be at least 6 months old.
  - The site must make it technically feasible to manage ads.

  Raptive's statement: "In a world full of AI-generated slop, we're proud to stand for the real, human web." — [PPC Land, Oct 19, 2025](https://ppc.land/raptive-drops-pageview-requirement-to-25-000-monthly-visits/); [Raptive help center, eligibility](https://help.raptive.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032840891-How-do-I-apply-to-Raptive) (blocked by Cloudflare when fetched; content confirmed via search summary)
- **Ezoic.** Core requirement is 250,000+ monthly active users. Sites already monetising with Ezoic before 19 Feb 2026 are grandfathered, but they lose that status if Ezoic is removed for more than 7 days. Google policy compliance and original content are also required. — [Ezoic Support, "Getting Started: Ezoic's Requirements"](https://support.ezoic.com/kb/article/getting-started-ezoics-requirements)
- **Ezoic access programmes.** Older guides saying Ezoic has "no minimum traffic" are outdated. One third-party source mentions an "Ezoic Access Now" programme for smaller sites, which is unverified. — [Newor Media, Ezoic alternatives after the 250K threshold](https://newormedia.com/blog/?p=1430) (search summary)
- **UK difference (consent).** Since 16 Jan 2024, publishers using AdSense, Ad Manager or AdMob must use a Google-certified CMP integrated with IAB TCF to serve personalised ads to users in the EEA and UK. Switzerland was added on 31 July 2024. Without one, only limited ads serve in those regions. — [AdSense Help, consent management requirements](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554116?hl=en)

### Inferences
- A realistic path for a new site: AdSense first, possibly from launch. Then Journey once there are 1,000 Tier-1 sessions in 30 days. Then Raptive at 25k pageviews with ≥50% Tier-1 traffic and a domain over 6 months old, or full Mediavine at about $5k a year in ad revenue.
- Both premium networks now explicitly screen for human, original, long-form content. Mass AI-generated pages put approval at risk.
- For UK visitors, the WordPress site needs a Google-certified TCF CMP before AdSense can serve personalised ads. Natura's ad slots should load only after consent in the UK.

### Gaps
- The exact Journey revenue share and the date it moved to 1,000 sessions are not confirmed on an official Mediavine page.
- The Raptive help page could not be fetched directly (Cloudflare block). Its requirements are confirmed via PPC Land and the search summary.
- US state privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA and others) and Google's US state-privacy settings were not researched.

## 3. Google Publisher Policies relevant to tone (calm self-sufficiency vs survival/prepper)

### Takeaway
Nothing in Google's Publisher Policies or Restrictions targets gardening, grow-your-own or self-sufficiency content. A calm, practical tone is fully ad-friendly. The risks arise from specific elements:
- harmful health claims, such as "this herb cures…" or anti-medical-consensus claims (Unreliable and harmful claims)
- sensational or clickbait framing (Deceptive practices / Misleading representation)
- fear or violence imagery (Shocking content)
- weapons, ammunition and explosives instructions, which are common in prepper content (Restrictions: fewer or no ads)
- growing recreational drugs (cannabis), which is a restricted category

No Google policy names "survival" or "prepper" content as such. Avoiding prepper content is a brand-safety and ad-demand choice, not a policy ban.

### Cited Findings
- **How policies are enforced.** Failure to comply "may result in Google blocking ads from appearing against your content, or suspending or terminating your account." — [Google Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10502938)
- **Unreliable and harmful claims.** Google does not allow content that "promotes harmful health claims, or relates to a current, major health crisis and contradicts authoritative scientific consensus" (examples: anti-vaccine advocacy). It also does not allow content that "contradicts authoritative scientific consensus on climate change". — [Google Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10502938)
- **Misleading representation.** Google does not allow content that "misrepresents, misstates, or conceals information about the publisher, the content creator, the purpose of the content, or the content itself". — [Google Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10502938)
- **Deceptive practices.** Google does not allow "enticing users to engage with content under false or unclear pretenses" or "promoting content, products, or services using false, dishonest, or deceptive claims" (e.g. "Get Rich Quick" schemes). — [Google Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10502938)
- **Dangerous or derogatory content.** This covers hate, harassment, threats or advocacy of harm to oneself or others, glorifying violence, and extortion. None of it applies to gardening content. — [Google Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10502938)
- **Animal cruelty.** "Promotes cruelty or gratuitous violence towards animals". This could matter only if the site expanded into livestock or slaughter content. — [Google Publisher Policies](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10502938)
- **How Publisher Restrictions work.** Restricted content may be monetised, but "fewer advertising sources will be eligible to bid", sometimes none, and "Google Ads ... advertisements will not serve on content labeled with these restrictions". — [Google Publisher Restrictions](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10437795)
- **Restricted categories.**
  - **Shocking content**: "gruesome, graphic, or disgusting accounts or imagery" (examples include "human or animal waste", "crime scene or accident photos"), depictions of violence, and "a significant amount of ... obscene or profane language".
  - **Explosives**: sale of explosives, or "instructions about the assembly, enhancement or acquisition of explosive items".
  - **Guns, gun parts**: sale of firearms, airsoft, BB guns, parts.
  - **Other weapons**: sale of weapons "promoted as products that can be used to injure an opponent in sport, self-defense, or combat", and instructions for them.
  - **Tobacco.**
  - **Recreational drugs**: "instructions on producing, purchasing, or using recreational drugs".
  - **Alcohol sale or misuse.**

  — [Google Publisher Restrictions](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10437795)

### Inferences
- **Prepper and survival content.** Typical topics are firearms and ammunition for self-defence, knives sold for "combat/self-defense", improvised explosives, fear-driven collapse scenarios, and "doctors don't want you to know" remedies. These map directly onto the Restrictions (guns, other weapons, explosives, shocking content) and onto the "unreliable and harmful claims" and "deceptive practices" policies.
  - Pages hit by Restrictions lose Google Ads demand, which is the main demand source behind AdSense RPM.
  - That is the policy rationale for avoiding the prepper angle.
- **Calm framing for self-sufficiency.** Phrases like "grow some of your own food", "a productive balcony" or "save on herbs" fall under none of these categories.
- **Things to avoid** in titles and pins:
  - fear framing ("when the grocery shelves are empty")
  - medicinal or cure claims for herbs or sprouts
  - homemade pesticide recipes that could be dangerous
  - home-growing of cannabis or tobacco (Restriction categories)
  - alcohol-making content (e.g. homemade fruit wine), which may reduce demand; a blogger notes that alcohol-forward sites see fewer advertisers ([search summary, Pittsburgh lifestyle blogger, via This Week in Blogging](https://thisweekinblogging.com/mediavine-vs-raptive/))
- **Composting and manure photos.** "Human or animal waste" imagery is listed under Shocking content. Composting and manure guides should use clean, non-graphic images. This is a cautious reading of the policy, not a documented enforcement case.

### Gaps
- No Google page mentions "prepper" or "survival" content explicitly, so the link is inferred from the categories above.
- The detailed "Learn more about unreliable and harmful claims" page URL I tried returned 404 (Google is migrating policies to the new Publisher Policies Help Center). The text quoted above is from the main policy page.
- Network-specific brand-safety rules (Mediavine/Raptive advertiser blocklists) are not public.
- No US/UK difference was found in the content policies themselves. The difference is consent (see section 2).

## 4. Google Search quality: YMYL, E-E-A-T, helpful content, and food safety (canning and preserving)

### Takeaway
- Since the March 2024 core update, "helpfulness" is assessed inside Google's core ranking systems rather than by a separate helpful content system.
- Google's guidance asks creators to show **who** made the content (bylines leading to author info), **how** (first-hand experience, sourcing) and **why** (to help people, not rankings).
- YMYL topics, those that can affect health or safety, "must be highly accurate and consistent with established expert consensus".
- Home canning is a food-safety topic where botulism is the risk. The authority is USDA / NCHFP (University of Georgia), and NCHFP advises using **only tested recipes** from the USDA, NCHFP or university extension.

### Cited Findings
- **March 2024 core update.** Google "enhanced our core ranking systems to show more helpful results using a variety of innovative signals and approaches. There's no longer one signal or system used to do this". The same post introduced spam policies on scaled content abuse (mass low-value pages "no matter whether content is produced" by automation or humans), expired domain abuse and site reputation abuse. — [Google Search Central Blog, March 2024 core update and new spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies)
- **Creating helpful, people-first content** (page last updated 2026-10-05).
  - Self-assessment questions include:
    - Does the content clearly demonstrate "first-hand expertise and a depth of knowledge"?
    - Does it present information with clear "sourcing, evidence of the expertise involved, background about the author or the site ... such as through links to an author page or a site's About page"?
    - "Is it self-evident to your visitors who authored your content? Do bylines lead to further information about the author[s]?"
  - Google "strongly encourage[s] adding accurate authorship information, such as bylines", while warning against "deceptive authorship information. Fabricating creator profiles".
  - Creators should consider explaining "How was this created?", including automation or AI use.
  - "For topics that could significantly impact people's lives or well-being (YMYL topics), the content must be highly accurate and consistent with established expert consensus."
  - Not all content needs specialised expertise; sharing personal experience can be enough.

  — [Google Search Central, Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- **E-E-A-T and ranking.** "While E-E-A-T itself isn't a specific ranking factor ... our systems give even more weight to content that aligns with strong E-E-A-T for topics that could significantly impact the health, financial stability, or safety of people ... YMYL." — [same page](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
- **The added "Experience" in E-E-A-T.** In Dec 2022, Google added "Experience" to E-A-T, asking whether content was "produced with some degree of experience, such as with actual use of a product". Rater guidelines "do not influence rankings directly". — [Google Search Central Blog, Dec 2022](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/google-raters-guidelines-e-e-a-t)
- **Latest Search Quality Rater Guidelines.**
  - The latest version verified is from **11 Sept 2025** (181→182 pages; the previous version was 23 Jan 2025).
  - It updated YMYL definitions: "YMYL Society" became "YMYL Government, Civics & Society".
  - It added examples for rating AI Overviews.

  — [Search Engine Roundtable, Sept 2025](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ads-related-searches-based-on-your-browsing-40092.html); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-updates-search-quality-raters-guidelines-adding-ai-overview-examples-ymyl-definitions-461908)
- **Claimed 2026 versions (unverified).** Marketing blogs claim QRG revisions in May and June 2026. PPC Land states that claims of a June 2026 revision "are not reflected in the published document". — [PPC Land, search quality rater guidelines](https://ppc.land/search-quality-rater-guidelines/); [pravinkumar.co](https://www.pravinkumar.co/blog/google-quality-rater-may-2026-webflow-saas)
- **NCHFP on tested recipes.** NCHFP "strongly advises using only tested recipes from reliable sources for all home canning. Sources include the USDA, NCHFP, and other university extension resources ... not all [online tips and recipes] are safe or accurate ... Using untested or unsafe methods can increase the risk of foodborne illness, particularly botulism, a potentially fatal illness." — [NCHFP, "Safe home canning: the importance of using tested recipes"](https://nchfp.uga.edu/newsflash/safe-home-canning-the-importance-of-using-tested-recipes)
- **NCHFP reference material.**
  - NCHFP hosts the **USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning (2015 revision)** and pages such as "Botulism: Home Canning Enemy #1" and "Dry Canning Raw Vegetables is an Unsafe Practice". It is hosted by the University of Georgia.
  - UGA Extension's *So Easy To Preserve* (6th ed., reviewed and updated 2020) is another reference.

  — [NCHFP general information](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/general/); [NCHFP newsflash](https://nchfp.uga.edu/newsflash/safe-home-canning-the-importance-of-using-tested-recipes)
- **Extension view of online recipes.** University of Wisconsin Extension: "Websites such as Pinterest, celebrity chefs ... cookbooks, magazines, and newspaper articles can not be counted on to share tested recipes." — [UW Extension, Barb Ingham, Feb 2019](https://winnebago.extension.wisc.edu/2019/02/20/14333) (search summary)
- **University of Maine study.** It reviewed 56 salsa-canning recipes from 43 food blogs and found that 70% did not include USDA guidelines in four key areas: acidification, thermal processing, contaminants and vacuum sealing. — [UMaine News](https://umaine.edu/news/?p=72747) (search summary; page not fetched due to TLS error, so the date is unconfirmed)

### Inferences
- **Topic risk.** Most container-gardening content (varieties, pot sizes, watering) is not YMYL. A few topic types sit in or near the health and safety YMYL area:
  - canning and preserving, fermenting, drying and oil infusions (botulism risk)
  - edible versus toxic plants (e.g. green potato parts, rhubarb leaves)
  - pesticide use on food plants
  - herbal or medicinal uses
- **For canning and preserving:**
  - Link to USDA/NCHFP or extension procedures rather than inventing recipes.
  - Do not publish "untested" methods such as oven canning, dry canning or open-kettle canning.
  - Add a visible note that it is a tested-recipe method, with a source and the reviewed date.
  - Consider omitting canning recipes entirely and linking out.
- **For trust**, consistent with Google's Who/How/Why guidance and the AGENTS.md rule "invent nothing":
  - a real byline that leads to an author page describing actual growing experience (real balcony, real climate zone)
  - original photos of the author's own containers and harvests
  - "How we tested" or "what we grew" notes
  - cited sources (extension services, RHS for the UK)
  - an About page and a visible last-updated date
  - no fabricated expert profiles
- **For the UK audience**, the RHS is the natural horticultural authority, but UK food-preservation guidance was not researched (e.g. the Food Standards Agency). USDA/NCHFP is the de facto reference for home canning, a practice more common in the US.

### Gaps
- I did not verify a Google-hosted copy of the QRG PDF directly. Its version and date come from SER and Search Engine Land reports.
- I found no Google statement specifically listing "home canning" as YMYL. The classification above is inferred from Google's definition (topics that "could significantly impact the health ... or safety of people").
- No UK government equivalent to NCHFP was researched.
- One blog claims NCHFP has had no new project funding since 2015. This is unverified and not included as a finding.
