# Demand and consumer trends: growing food in small and urban spaces (US first, then UK)

Research date: 23 October 2026. Most recent data comes first in each section. "Fetched" means the page or PDF was read in full. "Snippet" means only a search-result summary was seen, so check it before quoting it on the site.

## 1. How many people grow food, and in small or urban spaces? (US, then UK)

### Takeaway
About one US household in three grows vegetables. The share jumped in 2020 to about 41–42% and has been slipping back since, reaching 33% in 2025. Even so, gardeners are spending more than ever, and the most engaged groups are younger gardeners (Gen Y and Gen Z) and renters. In the UK, about a third of adults grow fruit or vegetables (33% in 2024, 36% in 2022), and young adults aged 16–24 are the most likely of the under-55s to grow edibles.

### Cited Findings

**US: National Gardening Association (NGA) / Garden Research**
- **2026 edition (published 19 May 2026, covering 2025):**
  - "About 4.5 million households left gardening in 2025".
  - Total spending nevertheless reached an all-time high of $79.0 billion (+13.5% over 2024).
  - Per-household spending reached a record $740 (+18.3%).
  - Source: [Garden Research, NGS 2026](https://gardenresearch.com/view/national-gardening-survey-2026-edition/) (fetched)
- The same 2026 edition reports a "renter inversion": renters out-purchase homeowners in vegetable and herb seed. This is a strong signal for small-space and urban food growing. — [Garden Research, NGS 2026](https://gardenresearch.com/view/national-gardening-survey-2026-edition/) (fetched)
  - The exact figures appear only in secondary sources: vegetable seed 43.5% of renters vs 36.6% of homeowners; herb seed 32.7% vs 22.0%. — [Modern Sprout, Home Growing Report](https://modernsprout.com/blogs/gardening/home-growing-report-how-americans-are-growing-more-at-home) (fetched; a secondary citation of NGS 2026)
- The 2026 edition has a section on the "impact of inflation and economic uncertainty on gardening participation". It also covers "Houseplants and container gardening" and "Edible gardening" as activity categories. The full figures are behind a paywall: the report and Excel workbook are sold; the bundle with What Gardeners Think costs $3,360. — [Garden Research, NGS 2026](https://gardenresearch.com/view/national-gardening-survey-2026-edition/)
- **Vegetable gardening in 2025:**
  - 33% of US households grew vegetables (about 44 million), down from 36% in 2024.
  - Vegetable-growing households spent $108 each on average (up from $85), for $4.7 billion in total.
  - Source: Greenhouse Management, citing NGA — [Greenhouse Management, "Why are gardeners purchasing more edible plants in 2026?"](https://www.greenhousemag.com/article/2026-horticulture-garden-trends-edible-plants-fruits-vegetables-growing/) (snippet only; the site blocked automated fetching)
- **Food-gardening households, 2018–2021 series (NGS 2022 edition, older data):**

  | Year | Share of US households food gardening |
  |---|---|
  | 2018 | 35.4% |
  | 2019 | 33.1% |
  | 2020 | 41.7% |
  | 2021 | 41.3% (about 53.7 million households) |

  Average food-gardening spend was $88.24 per household in 2021. — [FOX 9 / FOX TV Stations, citing NGA](https://www.fox9.com/news/over-2-in-5-us-households-now-growing-food-following-pandemic-boom) (fetched)
- **The 2020 pandemic boom (NGS 2021 edition, older data):**
  - 18.3 million people started gardening in 2020.
  - 42% of gardeners increased their activity because of the pandemic.
  - The intent to garden more was strongest among younger people, households with children, apartment and condo residents, and gardeners of colour.
  - Source: [FOX 9, citing NGA](https://www.fox9.com/news/over-2-in-5-us-households-now-growing-food-following-pandemic-boom) (fetched)
- **Older baseline:** the NGS 2016 edition found 36% of households food gardening, and 5 million of 6 million "new" gardening households were Millennials. This is pre-2020 data. — [Garden Center magazine](https://www.gardencentermag.com/article/national-gardening-survey-2016/) (snippet)

**US: Axiom Marketing, 2026 Gardening Outlook Study**
- The survey was fielded in September and October 2025 among 500 US homeowners who had bought garden plants. Note the sample: renters are excluded. — [Axiom 2026 Outlook PDF](https://axiomcom.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2026-Axiom-Gardening-Outlook-Survey.pdf) (fetched)
- **Overall activity:**
  - 47.6% spent more time gardening in 2025, the highest share since 2022.
  - 49.5% spent more money.
  - 63.6% (a record) plan to "plant more and expand my garden" in 2026.
  - Source: [Greenhouse Grower, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.greenhousegrower.com/management/annual-2026-gardening-outlook-study-reveals-trends-for-the-new-year/) (fetched)
- **By generation:**
  - More time gardening in 2025: Gen Y 66.7% and Gen Z 63.8%, against Gen X 33.1%.
  - Expect more time in 2026: Gen Y 66.7% and Gen Z 65.2%, against Gen X 37.2%.
  - Spent more money in 2025: Gen Y 61.1% and Gen Z 53.6%, against Gen X 43.0%.
  - Source: [Greenhouse Grower](https://www.greenhousegrower.com/management/annual-2026-gardening-outlook-study-reveals-trends-for-the-new-year/) (fetched)
- **Top garden projects for 2026:**
  - "Creating vegetable gardens" ranks second at 50.3%, after curb appeal at 60.1%.
  - 45.7% want "more blooms, fruits and vegetables", up 17.8% on 2024.
  - Further down the same chart: "Adding more container gardens" (26.2% / 22.3%), "Adding trellises and vertical gardening areas" (24.7% / 21.6%) and "Growing new kinds of vegetables and fruits" (about 28%).
  - Source: [Axiom PDF](https://axiomcom.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2026-Axiom-Gardening-Outlook-Survey.pdf) (fetched). The extracted chart text does not say clearly which percentage belongs to 2025 and which to 2026.
- **The year before (2025 Outlook):** respondents saying they spent more time gardening in 2024 fell by 10.9 points, the first such decline Axiom had recorded. — [LGR magazine / Greenhouse Grower snippet](https://www.greenhousegrower.com/?p=171719) (snippet)

**US: Frontdoor consumer survey (May 2025; commercial panel, less robust)**
- **Sample:** 992 US adults, renters included; 31% urban, 52% suburban, 17% rural.
- **Food-growing plans for 2025:**
  - 71% plan to grow a food garden in 2025: rural 78%, urban 72% ("embracing raised beds and container gardens"), suburban 68%.
  - 61% grew food in 2024.
  - 31% were trying food growing for the first time.
- **Garden types:** raised bed 47%, in-ground 45%, container 43%, indoor 27%, greenhouse 13%, community garden plot about 10%.
- Source: [Frontdoor](https://www.frontdoor.com/blog/gardening-landscaping/food-garden-trends-statistics) (fetched)
- These figures are far higher than NGA's 33%. This is an online panel of people answering a gardening survey, so treat it as indicative only.

**US: academic study**
- About 30% of respondents in a national study in *Public Health Nutrition* reported growing edible plants at home. — cited by [Modern Sprout](https://modernsprout.com/blogs/gardening/home-growing-report-how-americans-are-growing-more-at-home) (secondary; the original paper was not checked)

**UK**
- **HTA (YouGov for HTA, December 2024):**
  - 33% of UK adults grow their own fruit or vegetables.
  - 62% of adults with outdoor space use it to grow plants, trees or flowers.
  - Over-55s are the most likely to grow edibles in a private garden (38%).
  - 16–24-year-olds are more likely than any other under-55 group to grow edible plants.
  - Source: [HTA, "Gardening as a hobby amongst UK adults"](https://hta.org.uk/media/ba5fehgx/state-of-the-market-report-gardening-as-a-hobby-amongst-uk-adults.pdf) (fetched)
- **HTA / YouGov, October 2022:** 36% of UK adults use their garden or other outdoor space to grow vegetables, fruit or herbs. — [horticulture.co.uk, citing HTA State of the Market Jan 2023](https://horticulture.co.uk/gardening/statistics) (fetched; secondary)
- **Statista, 2021 (older data):** 28% of UK adults with a private garden had a vegetable patch; the survey had 2,000 respondents. — [horticulture.co.uk](https://horticulture.co.uk/gardening/statistics) (secondary)
- **ONS (2020):** about one British household in eight has no garden. This is the core "small-space / no-garden" audience. — cited by [horticulture.co.uk](https://horticulture.co.uk/gardening/statistics)
- **Allotments, APSE State of the Market 2025 (survey of councils, June–August 2025):**
  - 63% of councils report average waiting times of more than 18 months.
  - The share of councils with more than 1,000 people waiting is steady (31% by snippet).
  - 76% have cut standard plot sizes to create smaller plots, up 12 points since 2022.
  - APSE says the pressure "may be starting to plateau".
  - Source: [APSE press release](https://apse.org.uk/index.cfm/apse/news/articles/2025/a-positive-response-to-plot-size-adjustments-amid-acute-demand-for-allotments-new-apse-survey-reveals/) (fetched)
- **Allotment waiting lists, older figures:**
  - Over 174,000 people were on allotment waiting lists in 2023, with Bristol longest at 7,630. — [Gardens Illustrated](https://www.gardensillustrated.com/features/allotment-waiting-lists-2) (snippet; the original source was not confirmed)
  - The National Allotment Society (formerly NSALG) launched its first national council audit in 30 years. — [Chad](https://chad.co.uk/lifestyle/homes-and-gardens/national-allotment-society-launches-first-nationwide-audit-of-uk-plots-in-30-years-8505419) (snippet)
- **RHS State of Gardening Report 2025:** this YouGov survey covers children, not adults. About 60% of UK children (about 7 million) garden at least monthly, and growing fruit and vegetables is among their reasons. — [RHS PDF](https://www.rhs.org.uk/about-us/pdfs/about-the-rhs/mission-and-strategy/rhs-state-of-gardening-report.pdf) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **The headline US share fell after 2020, but small-space growing held up.** The headline participation figure is back to about pre-pandemic level (33% in 2025 against 33–35% in 2018–19). The segments that matter to Natura are holding or growing: renters buying vegetable and herb seed, and Gen Y and Gen Z engagement and spending. "Grow part of your food in a small space" fits these segments better than "the household garden" does.
- **The UK figure is steady.** A third of UK adults growing edibles (33–36%) has held from 2022 to 2024. Allotment scarcity, with waits often over 18 months, is a natural hook for "start on your balcony while you wait".

### Gaps
- I found no published national figure on container, balcony or patio food growing as such, in either the US or the UK. NGA has a "container gardening" category, but the numbers are paywalled.
- NGA participation by age and housing type for 2024–2025 is paywalled.
- I found no figure from USDA, AmericanHort or Scotts on home food growing for 2022–2026.
- I found no ONS figure on growing food.

## 2. Google Trends: shape of interest, 2020–2026

### Takeaway
Direct Google Trends queries were blocked during this research (HTTP 429). Published analyses agree on three points:
- Gardening searches peak every spring (April–May) and bottom out in December–January.
- Searches jumped sharply in spring 2020.
- Many generic "grow your own" terms then fell back below their peak.

The most recent analysis (May 2026) found small-space terms ("mini garden", "tabletop garden", "how to grow in a container") at all-time highs.

### Cited Findings
- **2020 surge:** global online searches for gardening rose 39% year on year during the 2020 lockdowns, citing Google data via Love the Garden. — [FloralDaily](https://www.floraldaily.com/article/9247540/gardening-sees-39-yoy-increase-in-global-online-searches/) (fetched headline)
- **Seasonality:** gardening search terms peak in April or May and are lowest in December or January. — [Greenhouse Grower, "What's most popular on Google — flowers or produce?"](https://www.greenhousegrower.com/management/retailing/whats-most-popular-on-google-flowers-or-produce/) (snippet)
- **UK, Sefton Meadows garden centre (2022):**
  - Method: Ahrefs, Semrush and Google Trends, comparing July 2022 with July 2019 to skip the pandemic surge.
  - Several gardening terms "hit their peak popularity in 2019, 2020 and 2021" and have since dropped.
  - "Grow your own and guerrilla gardening are taking a backseat, whilst no dig gardening and composting rise to the top."
  - "Small garden ideas" had about 27k average monthly UK searches in 2022.
  - Source: [Sefton Meadows blog](https://blog.seftonmeadows.co.uk/uk/garden-trends-popular/) (fetched)
- **US, Breck's (published Aug 2024):**
  - Analysed Google Trends for 38 gardening trends by state and found "food gardens" the most-searched trend nationwide.
  - Also named seed saving, composting, balcony gardens, hanging planters, ladder gardens and vertical gardening as popular.
  - Source: [Breck's](https://www.brecks.com/blogs/blog-post/popular-gardening-trends-by-state) (fetched; no numbers given)
- **24 Fingers, a UK social and SEO agency (10 May 2026):**
  - Small-space interest:
    - "Mini garden", "tabletop garden" and "small plants" were at all-time highs.
    - "How to grow in a container" spikes every spring and was "higher than ever".
    - Potatoes, then cucumbers, were the top trending "how to grow in a container" searches.
    - "Tabletop herb garden" was the top trending tabletop-garden search.
  - Month-on-month spikes:
    - "Mini greenhouse" +180% in a month.
    - "Apartment patio garden" +250% in a month.
  - Source: [24 Fingers](https://24fingers.co.uk/outdoor-garden-trends-2026/) (fetched)
  - Caveats: the analysis does not state its geography (US or UK), and its percentages are month-on-month spring rises, which seasonality inflates.

### Inferences
- The likely shape for "container gardening", "raised bed" and "grow your own food" is: a strong spring cycle every year, a 2020 spike, a fall in 2021–2022, then stable or rising seasonal peaks. Small-space and container terms appear to have recovered best. This is an inference from the sources above and should be confirmed in Google Trends by hand.
- An editorial calendar should publish container and balcony content in February and March, before the April–May peak.

### Gaps
- I could not get Google Trends curves for the requested terms in either country: "balcony garden", "vertical garden", "indoor vegetable garden", "hydroponics at home", "community garden", "seed saving", "preserving vegetables". The site owner should check these in trends.google.com, US then GB, over 5 years.
- I found no published analysis of "grow your own" searches during the UK cost-of-living crisis in 2022. Only general cost-of-living searches are documented, for example +1,590% for "cost of living" in the year to August 2022 per [Ciphr](https://www.ciphr.com/press-releases/the-cost-of-living-concerns-that-brits-are-googling-the-most) (snippet).

## 3. Which subtopics show the most demand?

### Takeaway
Few public keyword studies give volumes for these subtopics. The available signals point to these strongest subtopics:
- vegetable gardens and food gardens in general;
- containers, and specifically which crops grow in containers (potatoes, cucumbers, herbs);
- small-space and apartment patio ideas;
- tabletop and indoor herb gardens;
- mini greenhouses and raised beds;
- vertical and trellis growing;
- composting and seed saving.

### Cited Findings
- **Survey evidence, Frontdoor 2025:** methods used by US food gardeners are raised beds 47%, in-ground 45%, containers 43%, indoor 27% and community plots about 10%. The top crops are vegetables, then herbs, fruit and tubers. — [Frontdoor](https://www.frontdoor.com/blog/gardening-landscaping/food-garden-trends-statistics)
- **Survey evidence, Axiom 2026:** among homeowner project intentions, vegetable gardens score 50.3%. Container gardens and trellises or vertical areas each score about 22–26%. — [Axiom PDF](https://axiomcom.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2026-Axiom-Gardening-Outlook-Survey.pdf)
- **Search evidence:** the top trending small-space searches in spring 2026 were container crops (potatoes, cucumbers), tabletop herb gardens, apartment patio gardens and mini greenhouses. — [24 Fingers](https://24fingers.co.uk/outdoor-garden-trends-2026/)
- **Search evidence:** in UK searches compared with 2019, composting and no-dig gardening were rising while generic "grow your own" was falling. — [Sefton Meadows](https://blog.seftonmeadows.co.uk/uk/garden-trends-popular/)
- **Most-grown US vegetables:** tomatoes, cucumbers, sweet peppers, beans, carrots, summer squash, onions, hot peppers, lettuce and peas. This comes from NGA's 2014 five-year analysis (older data). — [FOX 9](https://www.fox9.com/news/over-2-in-5-us-households-now-growing-food-following-pandemic-boom)
- **Retail signal:** the most important plant traits in Axiom's survey were "produces lots of blooms, fruits and vegetables" (22.1%, down from 35.2%), "easy to grow" (18.9%) and "grows as well in containers as in the ground" (8.7%). — [Axiom PDF](https://axiomcom.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2026-Axiom-Gardening-Outlook-Survey.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Priority content clusters for Natura:**
  - "what to grow in containers", crop by crop (tomato, potato, cucumber, pepper, lettuce, herbs);
  - small-space and apartment patio layouts;
  - indoor and windowsill herbs and grow lights;
  - vertical and trellis growing;
  - "start while you wait for an allotment" (UK).
- **Secondary clusters:** composting in small spaces, seed saving and preserving the harvest. These show interest but lack volume data.

### Gaps
- I found no public Ahrefs, Semrush or Moz keyword volumes for any of these: "container gardening", "balcony garden", "square foot gardening", "grow lights", "hydroponics at home", "seed saving", "balcony composting", "rainwater balcony" or "preserving vegetables". The search tools return only general guidance on using them. Volumes need a paid keyword tool. Never invent them.
- I found no published SEO case study of a container or balcony gardening site with keyword data.

## 4. Effect of food prices and inflation (2022–2026) on interest in growing food

### Takeaway
Grocery prices are a strong stated motive for growing food. US surveys from 2025 show that about half of food gardeners want to cut their grocery bill. A January 2026 UK poll found 35% of shoppers had considered growing food because of food prices (47% of 18–34s). Inflation also cuts the other way: it pushed some households out of gardening altogether, with 4.5 million US households leaving in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **US (2026 NGA edition):** it analyses the "impact of inflation and economic uncertainty on gardening participation", including a "Great Middle-Income Exit" in the post-pandemic contraction. Spending is measured in real terms against cumulative CPI and PPI of +25.9% overall. — [Garden Research, NGS 2026](https://gardenresearch.com/view/national-gardening-survey-2026-edition/) (fetched)
- **US (2025 data, NGA via trade press):** households increasingly treat the food garden "as an economic hedge against rising grocery and input costs". — [Greenhouse Management](https://www.greenhousemag.com/article/2026-horticulture-garden-trends-edible-plants-fruits-vegetables-growing/) (snippet)
- **US Frontdoor survey (May 2025):**
  - 54% of 2025 food gardeners are "explicitly trying to lower their grocery bills".
  - 2 in 3 grow for high-quality produce.
  - Gardeners aim to cover about 23% of their grocery bill.
  - 36% grow food partly to offset tariff-driven price rises.
  - 11% of city gardeners say they grow food because they "can't afford to eat otherwise".
  - Respondents self-reported savings of $875 in 2024. This is not verified and should not be used as a claim on the site.
  - Source: [Frontdoor](https://www.frontdoor.com/blog/gardening-landscaping/food-garden-trends-statistics) (fetched)
- **US Axiom (fieldwork autumn 2025):**
  - Among respondents who spent less on gardening in 2025, groceries were the top use of the money (21.3%).
  - "Money/budget" was the main barrier to gardening more (33.5%).
  - Axiom's CEO says gardeners spent record time and money "in spite of economic uncertainty, job losses, and inflation".
  - Sources: [Axiom PDF](https://axiomcom.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2026-Axiom-Gardening-Outlook-Survey.pdf); [Greenhouse Grower](https://www.greenhousegrower.com/management/annual-2026-gardening-outlook-study-reveals-trends-for-the-new-year/) (fetched)
- **US (2023 NGA edition, 2022 data):** to cope with inflation, the most common household tactics were watering wisely, buying plants on sale and growing from seed. Over a third expected to spend more in 2023, especially on food gardening. — [Garden Research NGS 2023](https://gardenresearch.com/view/national-gardening-survey-2023-edition/) (snippet)
- **US Purdue Consumer Food Insights:** 84% of consumers called local grocery prices "high", and 44% said they produce or plan to produce some of their own food. — via [Progressive Grocer](https://progressivegrocer.com/are-inflation-weary-consumers-growing-their-own-food) (snippet; the exact survey month was not confirmed)
- **US 2026 prices:**
  - Retail fresh vegetable prices were 11.9% higher in May 2026 than in May 2025.
  - The USDA forecasts fresh vegetable prices to rise 7.7% in 2026 (prediction interval 4.5%–11.1%).
  - Source: [Modern Sprout](https://modernsprout.com/blogs/gardening/home-growing-report-how-americans-are-growing-more-at-home) (secondary; verify on the USDA ERS Food Price Outlook before use)
- **UK (published January 2026), MyBuilder.com:**
  - 35% of UK shoppers have considered growing their own fruit or vegetables because of higher food prices; nearly half (47%) of 18–34s have.
  - The same article says food prices rose by over a third in the five years to July 2025.
  - Source: [The Property Daily](https://www.thepropertydaily.co.uk/article/2026/01/30/millions-brits-feel-buying-food-too-expensive-here%E2%80%99s-money-saving-guide-growing) (fetched; a commissioned poll)
- **UK inflation context:** consumer prices rose 9.6% in the year to October 2022, the fastest rate in four decades. — [ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/articles/costofliving/latestinsights) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Saving on groceries is a real motive, especially among young urban adults, but quality and pleasure come first (2 in 3 Frontdoor respondents). This fits the site owner's positive angle: present growing food as better produce and a partial saving, never as survival.
- Make no savings claims (dollars or pounds) on the site. The only figures are self-reported in commercial surveys.

### Gaps
- I found no UK HTA or RHS survey that directly measures cost of living against grow-your-own uptake.
- I found no Google Trends evidence linking 2022–2023 inflation to "grow your own" searches.
- Seed-company sales data were seen only in news snippets (Baker Creek doubling in 2020, via [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/media/pandemic-high-grocery-lead-americans-gardening)) and are not verified.
